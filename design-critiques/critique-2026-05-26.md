## Visual Hierarchy

- **What draws the eye first (boot cold open):** wordmark center, then the green-amber checklist bottom-left. Correct. Boot reads as deliberate and on-brand.
- **What draws the eye first (settled SIM):** the operator stat numbers in the right panel (71, 70, 01) — they're the brightest, largest, most contrast-y elements. Globe markers come second. For a "global oversight" pitch this is debatable — you might want the GLOBE to win. The numbers are visually heavy because they're tabular bold on tinted card backgrounds.
- **What draws the eye first (zoomed-in LIVE):** the cluster of cyan ships in the Mediterranean. That's a *concentration* draw, not an intentional emphasis. Spreads attention rather than telling a story.
- **Reading flow (detail panel):** operator pill → vessel name → type subtitle → speed card → status card → cargo → vessel/flight details → position. This is now genuinely good. The pill at the top establishes "who," the name establishes "what," everything below answers "what's happening." Solid hierarchy.

## Accessibility

- **Color contrast (operator pill):** the cyan/violet text-on-tinted-background combo measures around 5:1 against the rounded chip's `bg-cyan-500/[0.08]` — passes WCAG AA at this text size. Good.
- **Color contrast (live marker on dark globe):** the muted live ship color `#527d8c` against the dark Mapbox `dark-v11` gray sits at roughly 3.2:1 against the ocean tile. Below AA for non-text but markers aren't text-spec — still, lowest end of the "noticeable" range. The Petros saturated cyan is ~7.5:1, much stronger.
- **Touch targets:** marker icons are 40px hit area (clickable wrapper hasn't shrunk). Plenty for desktop. The FILTER button in the sidebar at ~28px height is borderline for touch but fine for desktop.
- **Plane altitude in screenshot 7:** Norse Atlantic Airways N0503 at 3,530 m — that's ~11,600 ft, still suspect for cruise (typical Atlantic cruise is 35,000+ ft / 10,600+ m). The previous critique flagged the unit issue; still present.

## Priority Recommendations

*Ranked by demo impact in a 3-minute judge attention window.*

1. **Strengthen the Petros vs live visual differentiation** — currently failing the eye-test even though the palette spec is correct. Two surgical options that don't compromise legibility: (a) push live colors significantly more toward slate (`#3d6470` for ship, `#5e5670` for plane, `#7a6649` for truck — roughly halving the chroma again), OR (b) keep current live colors but add a subtle 1px dark outline `stroke: rgba(0,0,0,0.5)` only to PETROS markers as a "this is yours" affordance. (b) is more conservative and won't make live look broken.

2. **Soften the Mediterranean pile-up in zoomed-in LIVE view** — even with backend filtering, dense shipping lanes still cluster. Quick win: add JS-side proximity detection — if 3+ markers fall within 50px of each other, drop the lower-priority (unmatched commercial) ones' opacity to 0.35. Keeps the matched-operator ships prominent and lets the eye breathe.

3. **Lock the LIVE pill to a single color** (currently inherits accent from the selected vehicle's type) — small consistency win, big psychological one. The LIVE pill is a *data source* indicator, not a vehicle property. Match it to emerald to align with the boot-checklist "live" semantic, or to a neutral white/zinc.

4. **Fix the altitude unit handling for planes** — flagged in last critique, still showing low-thousands meter values that aren't cruise altitude. Either backend conversion bug or Aviation Edge providing feet labeled as meters. Hide altitude when < 1000 m to avoid showing fishy numbers.

5. **Sort sidebar scroll-to-top on deselect** — minor, but the "where did I land?" beat after pressing FLEET ← would be smoother if the list always returned to the top showing SHIPS / PLANES / TRUCKS headers.

6. **Consider whether the boot-overlay checklist hangs around long enough to be readable** — judges may not see the full status (MAPBOX / AIS / AVIATION / ROUTING / FLEET SYNC) before it dissolves. Adding ~200ms per line would let the story land.
